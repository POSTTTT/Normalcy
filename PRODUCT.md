# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

macOS (native SwiftUI and AppKit; menu bar app)

## Users

Keyboard-first Mac users who want to drive apps, desktops and windows without the mouse, in the spirit of Neovim. They open the command bar many times a day and visit Settings rarely, mostly to change a trigger, pick direction keys, or tune resize steps.

## Product Purpose

Normalcy is a keyboard command bar for macOS. Press a hotkey, type a short command, and it opens or switches apps, changes desktops, moves tabs into windows, snaps, resizes and scrolls windows. Success is muscle memory: commands that are short, predictable and customizable.

## Positioning

Vim-style short triggers (`h`, `l`, `re l`, `b saf`) typed into a native macOS bar, with every trigger and the direction keys remappable.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Settings window: tabbed (toolbar tabs), native macOS controls, saves immediately to UserDefaults.
- Every command can be renamed or switched off; direction keys (default h j k l) rename halves, thirds and quarters together.
- Command-bar keys (Enter, Shift+Enter, Tab, Ctrl+N/P, Esc, Ctrl+U, Ctrl+W, arrows) are fixed and listed for reference.

## Brand Commitments

- Settings look native to macOS; triggers and keys are drawn as key caps, matching the command bar's own key caps. (Confirmed by the user, 2026-11-04.)

## Product Principles

- Native first: a Mac user should trust every control on sight.
- Triggers are the product: make them the easiest thing to scan and edit.
- Short pages: one job per tab, no long scroll.
- Plain words: each setting says what it does in the user's terms.
